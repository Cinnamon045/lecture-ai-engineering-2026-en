To understand the difference between Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and fine-tuning, it helps to think about the difference between **giving a model an open-book exam** versus **studying to change the model's internal knowledge**.

According to the course glossary, **fine-tuning** involves further training a pre-trained model on specific data. It is particularly effective for teaching a model to adopt a certain tone, follow a specific output format, or handle behaviors in a specialized domain.

On the other hand, RAG functions by allowing the model to consult external data sources (like documents or databases) in real-time. As noted in the glossary entry for fine-tuning, when you need a model to have access to up-to-date facts or specific external information, using tools or RAG is generally more suitable than fine-tuning.

**A hint for your thinking:**
When deciding between the two, consider:
1. Does the model need to learn a new "style" or "personality" (which suggests fine-tuning)?
2. Or does the model need to look up accurate, changing information to answer a user's question (which suggests RAG)?

* Fine-tuning is covered in **Lecture 02**.
* RAG concepts are introduced in the context of augmenting model capabilities.
