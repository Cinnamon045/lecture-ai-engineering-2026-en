LoRA stands for **Low-Rank Adaptation**. 

Instead of training an entire massive model, which is computationally expensive, LoRA is a technique that keeps the original, pre-trained model weights "frozen." It then injects small, trainable matrices into the model architecture. 

To help you understand how it works, think about it this way: 
*   **Efficiency:** Rather than retraining billions of parameters, you only train these small, added matrices.
*   **Storage:** Because you aren't saving a whole new model, the resulting "adapters" are very small files that you can easily swap out.

When considering its effectiveness, think about the trade-off between the size of these small matrices (the "rank") and the ability of the model to learn new tasks. Why might a higher rank allow for better performance but consume more memory?
